Two lines that explain an everyday thing to a 10-year-old. Line 1 explains a queue with one vivid analogy — a caterpillar made of people. Line 2 says honestly what that analogy maps and what it leaves out. A judge model compares the entries two at a time.

## The entry

> A queue is a caterpillar made of people, shuffling forward one wiggle at a time whenever the person at the very front takes a step.
>
> The caterpillar shows how the whole line moves as one wave starting from the front, but it leaves out that a person can step out of line, cut in halfway, or overtake — a real caterpillar never lets one of its legs wander off on its own.

Why this angle: most queue analogies reach for ants or a train. A caterpillar is the one animal a 10-year-old can picture that moves exactly the way a queue does — as a single wave that starts at the front and rolls backwards, one segment at a time. The second line keeps the analogy honest by naming the exact place it breaks: people, unlike caterpillar legs, can leave the line whenever they like.
